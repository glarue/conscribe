Conscribe — Privacy Policy

Last updated October 20, 2026

Conscribe has no accounts and no user database. Your lists live on your devices. When they sync, they are encrypted end to end, and the server that passes them along cannot read them.

What is collected

Nothing that identifies you. There is no sign-up, no account, no email address, no phone number, and no profile. Nobody operating Conscribe has a list of who uses it, because no such list is created.

Your lists

Your lists are stored on your device. When you share a list with other devices in your household, they sync directly over your local network when those devices are together, and through a relay server when they are apart. In both cases the contents are encrypted on the sending device and decrypted on the receiving one.

The relay

The relay holds encrypted messages until the other device collects them. It is designed to know as little as possible, and what it can see is worth stating plainly:

  • It cannot read your lists. It receives bytes it has no key for.
  • Messages are addressed to identifiers derived from keys the relay does not have and cannot compute. The ones your household's devices use for routine syncing change each time the household's group keys advance (when a device joins or is removed, for example), not on a clock. The ones used to hand a new device its invitation are separate: a message there that nobody has collected is deleted after one day.
  • It does see the network address a message arrives from and the time it arrives, as any server does.
  • A message nobody collects is deleted after seven days. Some items are kept longer: an invitation that was collected, and larger transfers, are deleted after ninety days.

If you would rather not use the default relay, you can point the app at a different one, including one you run yourself.

Notifications

Conscribe is available for iPhone and for Android, and this one policy covers both.

Waking your other devices is on by default and can be turned off in Settings. While it is on, and once the device is in a household, a separate small service (the wake service) asks Apple (iPhone) or Google (Android) to wake your other devices. To do that the app sends the wake service this device's push token, which identifies the device to Apple or Google. When another household device changes the list, that device sends the wake service a sealed ticket for each device it wants to wake, plus one flag saying whether the notification should be visible or silent. The service can therefore tell that something was added to a list, but not to which list, by whom, or what it was. It never receives your lists, your household, or anything the relay holds. It keeps nothing on disk; what it holds in memory (a last-woken time per token and rate-limit counters) is lost when it restarts. It is deliberately a different program from the relay so that neither one holds both halves.

The message Apple or Google delivers has no list content: a fixed phrase for a visible notification, or nothing at all for a silent one. The wake service, Apple and Google can each see that a wake was sent to the device and when.

If you turn waking off, the app stops sending this device's token to the wake service, and tells your household's other devices to stop waking it. A ticket the service issued earlier is not recalled and remains usable until it expires. Turning it off does not stop your phone from obtaining a push token from Apple or Google when the app starts: that request is made at every launch whatever the setting, and the token is kept in memory and not sent anywhere while waking is off.

Diagnostics

The app keeps a diagnostic log on your device to help explain failures. It stays there. It records, among other things, your devices' names and what synced when; if you turn on verbose logging it also records item and list names. Nothing is uploaded on its own. There are two ways it can leave the device, both started by you. "Share diagnostics" hands the log to the system share sheet, and it goes wherever you choose to send it. "Upload log for support" asks first, encrypts the log on your device with a key that is created there and never sent anywhere, and uploads the encrypted log to the relay. Only someone you give the code shown on screen can read it.

Permissions

  • Camera (iPhone only) — only while the scanning screen is open, to read a household's invitation QR code when joining. Images are not stored and not transmitted. The Android app has no camera access; you paste the invitation link instead.
  • Location — optional, and off unless you turn on "Show Wi-Fi names" on the Local network page. Both iPhone and Android require location permission before an app may read the name of the Wi-Fi network, so Conscribe asks only then. It reads the network's name and never your position. The name is kept on the device and is not sent anywhere.
  • Local network — to find your other devices so they can sync directly instead of through the relay.
  • Notifications — optional, and only asked for when you turn on visible notifications.
  • Siri (iPhone) — optional. If you turn on Siri indexing, your list names are added to your iPhone's own search index so Siri can find them. They stay on the device.

Conscribe does not ask for your contacts or photo library.

No analytics, advertising, or tracking

There are no analytics, no advertising, and no trackers. The Android app includes Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging library, which receives push messages from Google; what that library itself sends to Google is determined by Google, and is covered by Google's privacy policy. The iPhone app uses Apple's own push service and includes no third-party software development kits. Conscribe does not contact any service other than the relay you have configured and, unless you have turned waking off, the wake service, plus Apple or Google for push as described above.

Deleting your data

Delete the app. There is no account to close and no copy held anywhere to request the deletion of. Anything still sitting on the relay expires on the schedule above.

Children

Conscribe is not directed at children, and collects no personal information from anyone regardless of age.
