Conscribe — Privacy Policy

Last updated November 5, 2026

Conscribe has no accounts and no user database. Your lists live on your devices. When devices sync, the lists are encrypted end to end, and the relay server cannot decrypt them.

What is collected

There is no sign-up, no account, no email address, no phone number, and no profile. Nobody operating Conscribe has a list of who uses it, because no such list is created. The relay and the wake service see network addresses and device tokens while they work; the sections below say what each one sees and keeps.

Your lists

Your lists are stored on your device. Devices in your household sync directly over your local network when they are together, and through a relay server when they are apart. A list shared with someone outside your household always syncs through the relay. In every case the contents are encrypted on the sending device and decrypted on the receiving one.

Purchases

Purchases are made through Apple or Google, which handle payment. Conscribe never receives your payment details, your receipt, or your store account. When a device unlocks Conscribe, it records in your household's encrypted data that it holds an unlock, which store it came from, and the purchase date. When a household starts its free trial, it records the day the trial started. The other devices in your household can see this; the relay cannot.

The relay

The relay holds encrypted messages until the other device collects them. It is designed to know as little as possible, and what it can see is worth stating plainly:

  • It cannot read your lists. It receives bytes it has no key for.
  • Messages are addressed to identifiers derived from keys the relay does not have and cannot compute. The identifiers your household's devices use for routine syncing change whenever the household's group keys change (when a device joins or is removed, for example), not on a schedule.
  • It does see the network address a message arrives from and the time it arrives, as any server does.
  • An undelivered message is deleted once its mailbox has gone seven days without a new message, or sooner if the relay restarts.
  • A change to who is in the household is posted once. If no device reads it, it is deleted after one day; once read, it is kept for ninety days.
  • The app deletes its sealed reply to an invitation when the invitation is used or expires after 24 hours. If that fails, the relay deletes it after ninety days.

If you would rather not use the default relay, you can point the app at a different one, including one you run yourself.

Notifications

Conscribe is available for iPhone and for Android, and this one policy covers both.

Letting your other devices wake this one is on by default. You can turn it off in Settings, Sound & alerts, "Let others wake this device". While it is on, and once the device is in a household, a separate small service (the wake service) asks Apple (iPhone) or Google (Android) to wake this device when another household device changes a list. To do that the app sends the wake service this device's push token, which identifies the device to Apple or Google, and a secret the app generates. When another household device changes a list, that device sends the wake service a sealed ticket for each device it wants to wake, plus one flag saying whether the notification should be visible or silent. A visible notification is asked for only when an item is added. The service can therefore tell that a list changed, and from the flag that an item was added, but not which list, by whom, or what it was. It never receives your lists, your household, or anything the relay holds. It keeps nothing on disk. What it holds in memory is lost when it restarts: for each token, the last time it was woken, whether Apple or Google reported the token invalid, and which Apple server it belongs to; and rate-limit counters. It is a separate program from the relay, so neither service sees both the push tokens and the encrypted messages.

The message Apple or Google delivers has no list content: a fixed phrase for a visible notification, and no text for a silent one. The wake service, Apple and Google can each see that a wake was sent to the device and when.

If you turn waking off, the app stops sending this device's token to the wake service, and tells your household's other devices to stop waking it. A ticket the service issued earlier is not recalled and remains usable until it expires. Turning it off does not stop your phone from obtaining a push token from Apple or Google when the app starts: that request is made at every launch whatever the setting, and the token is kept in memory and not sent anywhere while waking is off.

Diagnostics

The app keeps a diagnostic log on your device to help explain failures. By default it records faults, changes to who is in the household, and crashes. If you turn on verbose logging it also records what synced when and the names of items, lists and devices. Nothing is uploaded on its own. There are two ways the log can leave the device, both started by you, and either way it includes a table of your list names. "Share diagnostics" hands the log to the system share sheet (or copies it, if sharing is not available), and it goes wherever you choose to send it. "Upload log for support" asks first, encrypts the log on your device with a key that is created there and never sent anywhere, and uploads the encrypted log to the relay. Only someone you give the code shown on screen can read it.

Invitation links

An invitation is a link that starts with https://conscribeapp.com/j#. Tapping it opens Conscribe when the app is installed. When it is not, a page on conscribeapp.com explains what to do. The part after the # carries the invitation itself. A browser never sends that part to a website, so conscribeapp.com never sees the invitation and keeps nothing about it. (The app you send the link through, such as Messages or email, carries the whole link, as it carries any message you send.) What conscribeapp.com does see:

  • When a link is tapped, or when a message app or link-preview service fetches it to build a preview, the request reaches conscribeapp.com with the path /j and no invitation. As with any website, the server sees the network address the request came from.
  • Apple or Google fetches two small public files from conscribeapp.com (under /.well-known/) to check that Conscribe may open these links. Those requests carry no invitation.
  • The page at /j, shown when the app is not installed, stores nothing and sends nothing.

Permissions

  • Camera — only while the scanning screen is open, to read a household's invitation QR code when joining. The camera image is read on your device and is not stored or transmitted. You can always paste the invitation link instead.
  • Location — optional, and off unless you turn on "Show Wi-Fi names" on Settings, Sync & relay. Both iPhone and Android require location permission before an app may read the name of the Wi-Fi network, so Conscribe asks only then. It reads the network's name and never your position. The name is kept on the device and is not sent anywhere.
  • Local network (iPhone) — to find your other devices so they can sync directly instead of through the relay.
  • Notifications — optional. Asked for only when you turn on a notification setting, or when you create an invitation link, so the app can tell you when someone asks to join.
  • Siri and Spotlight (iPhone) — optional. If you turn on "Find your lists in Spotlight" (Settings, Advanced), your list names and item names are added to your iPhone's own search index so Siri and Spotlight can find them. They stay on the device.

Conscribe does not ask for your contacts or photo library.

No analytics, advertising, or tracking

There are no analytics, no advertising, and no trackers. Both apps are built with open-source libraries for the interface, networking, encryption and syncing. The iPhone app uses Apple's own push, purchase and search services and contains no third-party analytics, advertising or tracking code. The Android app also includes Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, Google Play services and Google Play Billing libraries, which talk to Google for push and purchases; what those libraries send to Google is set by Google and covered by Google's privacy policy. Conscribe does not contact any service other than the relay you have configured and, unless you have turned waking off, the wake service, plus Apple or Google for push and for purchases as described above.

Deleting your data

Delete the app to remove this device's copy. Other devices in your household keep theirs, and a CSV file you exported stays wherever you saved it. There is no account to close. Anything still on the relay is encrypted and expires on the schedule above.

Children

Conscribe is not directed at children, and collects no personal information from anyone regardless of age.
